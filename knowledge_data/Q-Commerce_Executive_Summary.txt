EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Understanding Q-commerce Adoption: The Role of Technological, Social,
Marketing Stimuli, and Service Quality on Usage Intention

Quick Commerce (Q-commerce) has emerged as one of the fastest-growing
segments of digital commerce, redefining consumer expectations through
ultra-fast delivery, convenience, and seamless shopping experiences.
Unlike traditional e-commerce, which typically delivers products within
several days, Q-commerce promises deliveries within 10–30 minutes,
making it particularly attractive for groceries, personal care products,
and everyday essentials. As competition intensifies among platforms such
as Blinkit, Zepto, and Swiggy Instamart, understanding what motivates
consumers to adopt and continue using these services has become
increasingly important.

This research investigates the key factors influencing consumers’
intention to use Q-commerce platforms. Specifically, it examines how
four external factors—technological stimuli, service quality, marketing
stimuli, and social stimuli—shape consumers’ perceived value and trust,
which ultimately determine their intention to use Q-commerce services.
The study employs the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R) framework,
where external stimuli influence internal psychological states, which
then lead to behavioural responses.

To test the proposed framework, the researchers conducted a quantitative
study using data collected from 795 consumers in Delhi and Gurugram,
India. Participants were selected using purposive sampling and surveyed
using a structured questionnaire measured on a five-point Likert scale.
The data were analysed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modelling (PLS-SEM), enabling the researchers to evaluate complex
relationships among multiple variables simultaneously.

The findings demonstrate that all four external stimuli positively
influence both perceived value and trust. Technological stimuli include
features such as intuitive mobile applications, secure payment systems,
AI-powered personalization, fast loading speeds, and real-time order
tracking. These technological capabilities not only improve the overall
shopping experience but also strengthen consumers’ confidence in the
platform. Among all technological attributes, security, platform
usability, and service reliability were particularly important in
building trust.

Service quality emerged as another critical determinant of Q-commerce
adoption. Consumers place significant importance on delivery speed,
product availability, order accuracy, responsive customer support, and
efficient refund processes. Since Q-commerce promises immediate
convenience, consistent operational performance directly enhances
perceived value while simultaneously reinforcing consumer trust.
Reliable execution of deliveries and effective issue resolution
encourage repeat usage and long-term customer relationships.

Marketing activities also play an important role in influencing consumer
behaviour. Promotional offers, flash sales, personalized
recommendations, bundled pricing, and loyalty programmes increase
consumers’ perception that they receive greater value for their money.
Furthermore, transparent pricing practices and honest promotional
communication strengthen trust by reducing uncertainty during purchasing
decisions. The study suggests that marketing strategies should focus not
only on attracting first-time users but also on maintaining long-term
engagement through personalized incentives.

Social stimuli represent the influence of reviews, ratings, customer
testimonials, peer recommendations, influencer endorsements, and online
community engagement. Consumers frequently rely on social proof before
making purchasing decisions, particularly in digital environments where
they cannot physically inspect products. Although social influence had a
statistically significant positive effect on both perceived value and
trust, its impact was comparatively weaker than technology and service
quality.

One of the study’s most important findings concerns the relationship
between perceived value and trust. Consumers who perceive greater
convenience, efficiency, affordability, and overall benefits from a
Q-commerce platform are more likely to develop trust in that platform.
Perceived value therefore functions as an important bridge between
external service characteristics and long-term consumer confidence.

The research further establishes that both perceived value and trust
positively influence consumers’ intention to use Q-commerce platforms.
However, trust emerged as the strongest predictor of usage intention.
While consumers appreciate fast delivery, attractive pricing, and
convenient services, their long-term adoption decisions depend more
heavily on confidence in the platform’s security, reliability,
transparency, and ability to consistently fulfil its promises.

The statistical analysis confirmed all eleven proposed hypotheses.
Technological stimuli, service quality, marketing stimuli, and social
stimuli each significantly enhanced perceived value and trust. Perceived
value positively influenced trust and directly increased usage
intention. Finally, trust exerted the strongest direct influence on
consumers’ intention to continue using Q-commerce platforms.

From a managerial perspective, the study recommends investing in
AI-powered personalization, cybersecurity, reliable delivery networks,
transparent pricing, loyalty programmes, verified customer reviews, and
responsive customer support. These initiatives strengthen trust,
increase customer satisfaction, and improve long-term adoption.

The study also contributes theoretically by extending the
Stimulus–Organism–Response framework to Q-commerce and demonstrating
that trust plays a more influential role than perceived value in driving
usage intention.

The research is limited by its focus on Delhi and Gurugram and its
reliance on self-reported responses. Future research should include more
diverse geographic regions, longitudinal studies, and emerging
technologies such as AI, sustainability, and blockchain.

Overall, the study concludes that technology, service quality,
marketing, and social influence all contribute to increasing perceived
value and trust. Among these factors, trust is the strongest determinant
of consumers’ intention to adopt and continue using Q-commerce
platforms. Companies that consistently deliver secure, reliable,
transparent, and customer-centric experiences are most likely to achieve
sustained adoption and customer loyalty.
